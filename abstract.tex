Volumetric video represents dynamic scenes in three dimensions (3D) and allows viewers to move through the captured environment with six degrees of freedom (6DoF). 
%
This capability can enable immersive telepresence, remote collaboration, and free-viewpoint live streaming. 
%
However, volumetric frames are substantially larger and more computationally expensive than conventional video.
%
Practical systems must compress and transmit this content at interactive frame rates, adapt to time-varying network bandwidth, and preserve visual quality while meeting stringent latency constraints.

This dissertation presents two complementary systems for enabling volumetric video conferencing and live streaming.
%
\livo supports bandwidth-adaptive, full-scene volumetric conferencing using arrays of RGB-D cameras. 
%
It reuses mature 2D video infrastructure to encode tiled color and depth streams, dynamically divides bandwidth between them, and predicts and culls content outside the receiver's view. 
%
\gsnfs is a GPU-resident post-training codec for dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (\dgs) representations and point clouds. 
%
It reformulates octree and attribute coding for parallel execution and improves compression through color decorrelation, enabling native 3D encoding and decoding at frame rate.
%

Evaluations show that \livo sustains approximately 30~fps with around 250~ms of end-to-end latency while improving visual quality over the evaluated alternatives. 
%
\gsnfs meets a 30~fps encode and decode budget on desktop GPUs, decodes some sequences at up to approximately 25~fps on a mobile-class GPU, and also operates as a frame-rate point-cloud codec. 
%
Together, these systems demonstrate an immediately deployable path to immersive conferencing and a native 3D compression path for future volumetric streaming systems.
