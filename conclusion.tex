\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work} \label{ch:conclusion}

This dissertation explores how 3D data can be elevated from a local sensing artifact to a shared medium supporting collaborative scene understanding, perception, and immersive vehicular experiences. By designing and evaluating systems that span the full 3D data pipeline -- \textbf{reconstruction, interpretation, and delivery} -- this dissertation demonstrates how vehicles and infrastructure can collaboratively sense, understand, and experience the world through rich spatial representations.

Across \recap, \cip, and \mars, this dissertation makes three overarching contributions.

\parab{3D Scene Reconstruction.} The dissertation demonstrates that vehicles equipped with commodity 3D sensors can opportunistically contribute to cloud-based 3D traffic reconstruction. It introduces algorithms and system techniques that fuse sparse, and dynamic views into a merged view with high geometric accuracy. These results show that large-scale 3D reconstruction is feasible even under dynamic and uncontrolled vehicular conditions.

\parab{Real-time Infrastructure-assisted 3D Perception.} The dissertation presents methods that enable cooperative infrastructure perception at real-time latencies. By combining precise geometric alignment, reuse of visual abstractions, and hardware-aware system optimizations, it achieves accurate detection, tracking, and motion estimation. This work demonstrates that cooperative infrastructure perception can enhance safety, increase traffic throughput, and complement on-board vehicle perception.

\parab{Adaptive 3D Content Delivery of In-vehicle AR.} Toward enabling the next-generation of in-vehicle AR, this dissertation demonstrates how high-quality 3D video can be reliably streamed to moving vehicles despite unpredictable network bandwidth. Through adaptive quality control, motion-aware compression, and predictive prefetching, the system delivers spatially coherent AR experiences with minimal playback stalls.

Together, these contributions advance the thesis that 3D data should be treated as a first-class, networked medium that flows across vehicles, infrastructure, and cloud systems to support both automated decision making and immersive in-vehicle experiences.

\section{Future Directions}

While this dissertation demonstrates how 3D data can be reconstructed, interpreted, and delivered as a shared medium in connected vehicular systems, it also opens up several promising directions for future research. As 3D sensing technologies, vehicular connectivity, and in-vehicle displays continue to evolve, new opportunities arise to further expand the scalability, robustness, and impact of networked 3D data processing. This section outlines key directions that build upon the systems and insights developed in this dissertation.

\parab{Scalable 3D Scene Reconstruction.} \recap demonstrates that 3D views from moving vehicles can be opportunistically fused to reconstruct the traffic scenes with high fidelity. A natural extension is to scale this reconstruction across larger spatial regions and longer temporal horizons. Integrating static information such as pre-built high definition maps of the region or selectively incorporating infrastructure sensors around the scene could further improve coverage and long-term stability while reducing the latency.

\parab{Adaptive and Scalable Infrastructure Perception.} \cip shows that cooperative multiple infrastructure LiDARs can provide accurate and low-latency traffic perception when processed in the cloud. Future research could explore adaptive perception pipeline that can dynamically scale with traffic density, sensor availability, and application requirements. For example, cloud could be elastically allocated based on traffic dynamics, or perception fidelity could be adjusted depending on application demands. Extending perception beyond vehicles to support pedestrians or city operators also presents new opportunities for broader situational awareness.

\parab{Richer Human-Centered 3D Experiences in Vehicles.} \mars demonstrates the feasibility of streaming spatially coherent 3D video to moving vehicles. Future work could expand this direction by incorporating user interaction, multi-user shared experiences, or tighter integration with real-world context. Advances in display hardware, eye tracking, and perceptual modeling could enable adaptive rendering techniques that further optimize bandwidth and rendering quality.

\parab{End-to-End Closed Loop Networked 3D Systems.}
This dissertation treats reconstruction, interpretation, and delivery as interconnected but modular stages. A promising direction is to move beyond this modular separated design toward end-to-end systems in which these stages are jointly optimized through feedback. In such systems, downstream requirements (e.g., perception accuracy or AR rendering fidelity) could guide how 3D data is sensed, fused, and prioritized, while upstream limitations in sensing or networking could dynamically shape interpretation and delivery. Developing closed-loop networked 3D systems would require new abstractions that integrate sensing, streaming, and cloud processing.

\parab{Multi-modal and Heterogeneous 3D Sensing.} This dissertation primarily focuses on LiDAR-based 3D sensing due to its geometric accuracy and robustness in traffic environments. A promising direction for future work is to extend these systems to incorporate heterogeneous sensing modalities including cameras, stereo cameras, and depth cameras. Fusing complementary modalities can improve robustness under adverse conditions such as sparse LiDAR coverage, occlusions, or poor weather, while also reducing sensing and deployment costs.
